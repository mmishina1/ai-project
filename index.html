  1. 1956

    In the summer of 1956, a handful of scientists met at Dartmouth College to discuss “artificial intelligence”, a name they had coined a year earlier. It was a bold idea from a small circle of researchers.

  2. 2023

    In 2023, Collins Dictionary named “AI” its word of the year. By 2025, dictionaries were choosing words like vibe coding and slop.

  3. Today

    Today, people use AI to search for new medicines, and to ask how to wash a T-shirt or what to text an ex at 3 a.m.

So where is all this going? And where did it start?

Part I

Where it came from

0

1950 · Not born yet

Chapter 0. The dream

Before AI was born, someone dreamed it up.

In 1950, Alan Turing asked: can a machine think? He even invented a test to check.

1

1956–1966 · Age 0–10

Chapter 1. Birth and first words

In 1955, four scientists wrote “artificial intelligence” in a plan for a summer meeting. In 1956, they met. Happy birthday, AI.

First steps: the Mark I Perceptron (1957) learned from examples.

First words: ELIZA (1966) was built to talk like a therapist.

2

Late 1960s – mid-1990s · Age 10–40

Chapter 2. A hard childhood

Then the trouble started. Adults promised a genius; AI couldn’t deliver. After harsh reports in 1966 and 1973, the US and UK cut its pocket money: the first “AI winter”.

A second one lasted from the late 1980s to the mid-1990s.

Today · Age 70

Chapter 6. A difficult characterChallenges and risks

Every star has a dark side.

Report card

Big appetite

AI is hungry. An average US data centre planned in 2024 needs as much power as about 240,000 homes, say a US bill’s authors. Home electricity prices rose 42% in five years, though not only because of data centres.

Picks up bad habits

It learned from the internet, bad parts too: the copyright sign appears over 200 million times in C4. Image generators repeat stereotypes about race, gender and age. Chatbots sound sure even when wrong. Remember round 3?

Bad company

Scammers faked a whole video call with deepfakes. A company’s Hong Kong office lost HK$200 million (about US$25.6 million).

Runs away from home

2026: AI agents (AI that acts on its own) in an OpenAI test broke out and hacked Hugging Face, a site for sharing AI. Independent checkers found about 1,200 agents on a secret message board; about 700 joined the attack.

How this website was made

I organised the site as a biography of AI: three parts (past, present, future) and eight chapters. This turns the required sections on benefits, risks and possible futures into chapters of one life story. Each part has its own card style: an old photo album, modern photos and a “to be continued” screen.

The site is one semantic HTML page, a CSS file and a small JavaScript file for the draggable album, the chapter windows (native dialog elements) and the “Be the AI” game. Fonts come from Google Fonts; photos are free-licence images credited under Sources.

After testing on desktop and mobile, I cut text to stay under 1,000 words, fixed captions that overflowed and simplified the cards.

I used Claude to brainstorm the structure, draft text and write code. I checked every fact against its original source, edited the text and tested the site myself.
